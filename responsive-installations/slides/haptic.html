Output 2 · Buzzer

A buzzer needs more current than a pin can give, so a transistor switches it.

GPIO 13 (pin 33) through 1kΩ to a PN2222A transistor. Buzzer from 5V to the transistor, with a diode across it.

[Fritzing diagram: buzzer + transistor on GPIO 13]

from gpiozero.tones import Tone
buzzer = TonalBuzzer(13, octaves=3)

hz is how high the note is, ms how long it lasts. With octaves=3, TonalBuzzer plays 55 Hz to about 3.5 kHz; a small buzzer is loudest around 2 to 2.7 kHz. Sound is where outputs get expressive: try a two-note chime, or a rising sweep as someone walks up. Use a passive buzzer: an active one has its own oscillator and only beeps at one pitch. Never wire the buzzer straight to a GPIO pin — it draws more than the pin is rated for. The diode soaks up the voltage spike when the buzzer switches off (stripe towards 5V).
