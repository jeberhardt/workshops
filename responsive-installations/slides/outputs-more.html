More outputs to explore

LED strips

Addressable pixels, each its own colour

Relay

Switch lamps, fans or a fog machine

DC motor

Spinning at any speed, through a driver

Servo

Point to an angle and hold it

Stepper motor

Precise, repeatable turns for slow kinetics

Solenoid

A sharp knock, tap or strike

Sound

A buzzer’s tones, or speakers from the sketch

Fan

Moving air across skin or fabric

Small display

OLED or e-ink text on the object itself

Projector

The sketch on walls, buildings, sculpture

DMX lighting

Drive venue stage lights via USB-DMX

OSC or MIDI

Hand off to TouchDesigner, Ableton, a lighting desk

Same pattern as the LED and haptic motor: the sketch sends a message, Python drives the hardware. Anything with a motor or a coil (DC motor, solenoid, fan) needs a transistor or driver and its own power, like the haptic motor. For relays, use pre-built low-voltage modules or smart plugs — never wire mains power in a workshop. LED strip libraries differ between the Pi 4 and Pi 5, so check before buying.
