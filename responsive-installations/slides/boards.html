Whatever the board, it sends the same messages, so your sketch never changes.

Not hands-on today, just so people know the option exists. The Pi stays the brain: it runs the bridge and the sketch. Extra boards are just more hands. An Arduino prints lines over its USB cable and the bridge reads them; an ESP32 connects over Wi-Fi and sends messages to the bridge like the sketch does. Either way the messages look the same, so the p5.js side doesn’t change. Arduino Uno is 5V, so 5V sensors like the HC-SR04 connect without a voltage divider. The ESP32’s ADC is noisier and less linear than a dedicated chip, so smoothing matters even more there.
